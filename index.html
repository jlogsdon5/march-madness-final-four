A MARCH MADNESS ANALYSIS · FOR A COLLEGE BASKETBALL ANALYST

How did Final Four rates differ for seeds 1–4 versus 5–16—and which lower-seeded programs broke through?

Men’s NCAA tournament · All selected team-season entries, including First Four participants · 2015–2025, excluding canceled 2020

By Jake Logsdon

Teams seeded 1–4 reached the Final Four in 29 of 160 entries (18.13%), compared with 11 of 520 entries (2.12%) for seeds 5–16. The higher-seeded group advanced at about 8.6 times the rate, while 11 different lower-seeded programs broke through. For your tournament preview, anchor expectations in the seed-group base rates and use past surprise runs as examples of possibility, rather than evidence that a particular program will repeat.

01 / THE BASE RATE

A top-four seed carried an 8.6× higher Final Four rate.

Seeds 1–4 accounted for 29 of the 40 Final Four appearances. Their entry-level success rate was 18.13%, versus 2.12% for seeds 5–16. These rates use each group’s own tournament entries as the denominator; they are not shares of Final Four places.

02 / THE EXCEPTIONAL YEAR

In 2023, lower-seeded teams took three of the four spots.

Miami FL and San Diego St., both seeded 5, joined 9-seed Florida Atlantic in the 2023 Final Four. That was the period’s largest annual total for seeds 5–16. In contrast, 2025 had four 1-seeds and no teams seeded 5–16 in the Final Four.

03 / THE BREAKTHROUGH PROGRAMS

Eleven breakthroughs came from eleven different programs.

No program reached the Final Four more than once as a seed 5–16 during this period. Loyola Chicago (2018), UCLA (2021), and North Carolina St. (2024) each did so as an 11-seed, the lowest seed represented in these breakthroughs. The record supports discussing surprises broadly, rather than naming a repeat Cinderella specialist.

THE DATA / DEFINITIONS FIRST

What we count—and what we don’t.

Source: Nishaan Amin, March Madness Data (Kaggle), using KenPom Barttorvik.csv from the supplied archive. One row is one team selected for one tournament. We retain all entrants, including teams eliminated in the First Four, and restrict the analysis to 2015–2025. There was no tournament in 2020. We exclude all other seasons, including 2026, whose outcomes are unfinished in this archive. We retain only the seven columns needed for this analysis; no player or coach names are used.

The resulting file contains 680 entries across ten tournaments and 217 distinct team labels, with no duplicate team-season rows or missing selected fields. Each included year contains 68 entries and four Final Four teams. These checks establish internal consistency; they do not constitute an independent audit of every source result. Team names follow the source.

Tournament entries
Count the included team-season rows.
Final Four appearances
Sum Final_Four, coded 1 when Finish_Round is 1 (champion), 2 (runner-up), or 4 (semifinalist); otherwise 0.
Final Four rate: seeds 1–4
Final Four appearances among entries seeded 1–4 divided by the number of entries seeded 1–4.
Final Four rate: seeds 5–16
Final Four appearances among entries seeded 5–16 divided by the number of entries seeded 5–16.

Percentages are calculated before rounding and displayed to two decimal places. A group with no selected entries displays N/A. The dashboard applies its year and team filters to every metric and chart. Its program chart shows counts, not program success rates. Seeds and historical outcomes describe this sample; they do not establish causation or guarantee future results.
